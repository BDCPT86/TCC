01

Getting Started & PIN Login

The TCC Scorer is a Progressive Web App (PWA) — it runs in the browser and can be installed to your home screen for a native app feel. No download from an app store required.

Installing to Home Screen

On iOS (Safari): tap the Share icon → Add to Home Screen. On Android (Chrome): tap the menu → Install App. Once installed, the scorer opens full-screen with the TCC tiger icon.

⚠️

iPhone users: use Safari, not Chrome. Apple only allows the offline cache to run in Safari and in apps added to the home screen. In Chrome, Firefox or Edge on an iPhone the scorer has no offline storage at all — if signal drops mid-match you lose the scoring. Open the scorer in Safari and add it to the home screen. On Android any browser is fine.

PIN Login

When you open the app you'll be asked to enter your scorer PIN. This is a 4-digit code assigned by your club administrator. The PIN grants access to the fixture list and match scoring.

Animations

The same screen has an Animations switch. On, the score bumps as it changes, the board flashes gold for a boundary and red for a wicket, the ball you just scored lands in the over strip, and the batters' cards slide when the strike changes — confirmation that a tap went in without having to read the numbers. Off, everything appears instantly. Scoring works the same either way, and buttons respond immediately in both. If your phone is set to reduce motion, the app follows that unless you switch it on here.

Four panels

Each section folds. Tap a heading to open or shut it, and the heading carries a count so the screen tells you what is there without opening anything.

Being scored now

Any match still marked as in progress shows here, in its own panel at the top of the screen, whatever date it carries — a green edge, the score so far and which innings it is in. Tap it and enter the match PIN to carry on scoring it, exactly as when you take a match over from another phone.

💡

This is the way back into a match whose fixture date has passed, or one with no fixture behind it at all. Fixtures from the last two weeks that were never scored also stay in the list under today's, so a rained-off or wrongly dated game is still one tap away.

Recent Results & Date Range

Open the Recent results panel to reach this. It defaults to the last 30 days. Use the From and To date pickers to search further back, then tap Go to reload.

💡

Tapping a completed match opens its full scorecard — batting, bowling and fall of wickets — with ⬇ PDF to download it and ✎ Names to correct a misspelt player.

Tapping a fixture opens the Setup screen. Fill in these details, then tap Start Scoring →.

It is in two panels. Match is open — the overs, the toss and who bats first, which you set every game. Rules is folded away, because its settings change once a season rather than once a match: the format, the bowler limit, what a wide costs and whether extras use up a ball. Tap the heading to open it.

Each heading carries a summary of what is inside it, so you can check the rules without opening anything — wd/nb 2 runs · extras use a ball. Only settings that differ from the ordinary game appear there, so a quiet Rules heading means nothing unusual is set. Whichever panels you leave open are remembered on that phone.

Bowler limits

When an over ends, the bowler list shows how much each bowler has left, e.g. 3 ov left. Anyone who has bowled their full allowance is shown greyed out under Bowled their limit and can't be picked — if you try, the app tells you why. If overs are reduced for rain, the limit adjusts on its own (a 23-over innings allows 5).

Opening Players

After the PIN, the app asks for the two opening batters and the opening bowler. The names you type are remembered for each side and offered back later in the match, so spell them the way you want them on the scorecard — though you can correct a name afterwards if you need to.

Wides, no-balls and the penalty

Wide / no-ball penalty sets what an illegal delivery costs before any runs taken off it: 2 runs in a 100-ball match, 1 in every other format. Change it if your competition differs — it applies to wides and no-balls alike, and to the bowler's figures.

Wides & no-balls use up a ball

Some competitions do not re-bowl an illegal delivery — the run is given but the ball still counts, so an innings is exactly its allotted number of balls. The switch on the setup screen turns that on. It starts on for 100-ball matches and off everywhere else; change it before you start and the app remembers your choice for that match.

  • On: a wide is 1 run and one of the 100. A no-ball is 2 runs, one of the 100, and still brings a free hit.
  • The last ball of the innings is the exception — it must be legal. A wide or no-ball there is re-bowled, as many times as it takes, so an innings never ends on an illegal delivery.
  • Off: every wide and no-ball is re-bowled, as in the Laws.
💡

Balls faced on the batting card do not change: a batter is credited with facing a no-ball, never a wide. The bowler's figures do count the delivery, so his ball count matches what he actually bowled.

100

The Hundred — 100-ball matches

Choose The Hundred (100 balls, 5-ball sets) on the Match Setup screen. The overs field disappears — an innings is always 100 balls. Everything below applies only to this format; overs matches are unchanged.

⚠️

A set is not an over. Bowlers change every 5 balls, but the batters only change ends every 10 balls. So after the first set of a pair, the same batter is still on strike. The app handles this — do not swap the batters yourself.

What is different

1
Sets of 5, not overs of 6. The scoreboard counts balls, not overs — you will see 63/100 balls rather than 10.3 ov. An innings is 20 sets.
2
Ends change every 10 balls. Confirm the end of each set as normal; the app swaps the batters only when the ends actually change.
3
A bowler may bowl two sets in a row. When the set ends, the picker offers “… — bowl 2nd set” at the top. Tap that if the captain keeps them on; otherwise choose someone else. After two consecutive sets they must be replaced.
4
20 balls per bowler, maximum. Anyone who has bowled their 20 is shown greyed out under Bowled their limit and can't be picked. Figures show as balls, e.g. 20b-0-31-2.
5
Powerplay is the first 25 balls. A blue strip counts down the balls remaining. Only two fielders are allowed outside the circle during it — the app does not police fielding positions, it only tells you when the powerplay is on.
6
A no-ball is worth 2 runs (not 1), plus a free hit. Score it with the NB button exactly as normal — the app adds the right penalty for the format.
7
Run rate is per ball. The Hundred’s official measure is runs divided by balls faced, so a fast innings reads RR 1.67/b, not 10 an over. The /b suffix marks it. Bowler economy uses the same basis, and so does the chase bar: Need 32 off 22 balls · RRR 1.45/b.

Rain and short matches

🌧

At least 25 balls must be bowled to the side batting second for the match to stand. Below that, the win/lose options are hidden and the only choices are No Result or Abandoned. If the chase finishes early — target passed or all out — that is a result regardless of how few balls were bowled.

💡

Score a practice match before the tournament. The two things worth seeing at least once are the bowl 2nd set prompt and a wicket on the last ball of a set — those are the moments where hesitation costs you.

04

Scoring Screen Overview

This is the real scoring screen, part-way through a chase. Everything is in the same place every ball, so it's worth knowing the six zones from top to bottom.

① Score panel

The score, overs, run rate and extras. In a chase a gold bar shows Need X off Y balls and the required rate.

② Batters

The striker is outlined. ⇄ swaps strike. The partnership sits between them and counts every run the pair has added, extras included. Tap a batter to retire him or record a timed out.

③ Bowler and this over

The bowler and his figures on one line — tap it to change bowler. Underneath, every delivery in the over (wides and no-balls too), then an empty box for each legal ball still to come; the next one is outlined. Tap a ball to edit it.

④ Run pad

0 1 2 3, then 4, 6 and a wide W. Below: Wd Nb B Lb, then Undo, Other · 5 and ✎ for commentary.

⑤ Shot (optional)

Where the ball went: Off, Cvr, Str, Leg or Fine.

⑥ Bottom bar

📋 Card for the scorecard, History for past overs, End to end the innings or match.

💡

Long names are shortened on this screen only. If a name doesn't fit, it shows as initials and surname (C Oosthuizen, A van der Westhuizen). The scorecard, PDF and website always show the full name.

ℹ️

If your phone's text size is set large, the screen scales with it. On a very small phone, scroll down a little to reach the Shot row.

Commentary

Tap ✎ to open a small box, type a note, then score the ball — the note is attached to that ball, shows in the ball-by-ball view, and the box folds away again. Nothing else changes if you never use it.

Shot direction (optional)

The Shot row under the pad records where the ball went: Off / Cvr / Str / Leg / Fine. Tap the direction first, then the runs — the direction is attached to that ball and clears itself afterwards. Skip it whenever you're busy; it never holds up scoring.

💡

The more balls have a direction, the richer the analysis later — but a ball without one is perfectly fine.

Wide (Wd)

Tap Wd. A run picker appears — select how many runs were scored off the wide (1 is the default penalty, add any additional runs). Does not count as a legal ball. Strike swaps if an odd number of wide runs are scored.

No Ball (NB)

Tap NB, then select how many runs the batter hit off the delivery (0–6). A second step asks if anything additional happened off the no-ball:

  • None — just the no-ball penalty + bat runs
  • Bye / Leg-bye — extra runs added to byes/leg-byes total
  • Overthrow — additional overthrow runs
  • Penalty — 5-run penalty added to penalty extras

The ball chip will show the full detail, e.g. Nb+3+2B. Does not count as a legal ball. If Free Hits are enabled, a Free Hit banner will appear for the next delivery.

💡

Where the runs go. The no-ball penalty goes to extras and never to the batter. Only runs hit off the bat are credited to the striker. Byes or leg-byes taken off a no-ball go to the byes column and are not charged to the bowler — the bowler is charged the penalty plus anything off the bat. If the batters ran an odd number, strike rotates as normal.

Bye (B) / Leg-bye (LB)

Tap B or LB, then select the number of runs. These count as legal deliveries and are added to the byes/leg-byes extras column, not the batter's score. The batters still ran them, so an odd number rotates the strike.

Other · 5

The Other · 5 button, next to Undo, holds the less common entries:

  • 5 runs — five runs off the bat, all run. One tap, recorded straight away.
  • Overthrow — extra runs from a fielding error, credited to the striker.
  • Penalty runs — awarded by the umpire; they go to extras only.
  • Match tools — Reduce overs (rain) and Catch up the score. See Ending an innings.

Every one of these, and every wide, no-ball, bye and leg-bye, counts towards the current partnership — so the partnerships always add up to the total.

💡

The chip label on the over row shows exactly what happened — Wd NB B LB — so you can glance back at any ball in the over.

Dismissal types

  • Bowled, Caught, Caught & Bowled, LBW, Stumped, Hit Wicket — the bowler's wicket
  • Run Out — not the bowler's. Asks which batter was out
  • Obstructing (obstructing the field — this now includes the old "handled the ball") — not the bowler's. Either batter can be out, so it asks which
  • Hit twice (hit the ball twice) — the striker, not the bowler's
  • Retired Hurt — not out; he can come back later
  • Retired Out — a wicket, not the bowler's

When TCC is fielding, Caught, Run Out and Stumped also ask for the fielder, so the scorecard reads c Fortuin b Miller or run out (Guly).

Run out or obstruction at the non-striker's end

Choosing Run Out or Obstructing shows a Which batter was out? choice. Pick the non-striker and he is the one dismissed — the new batter comes in at that end, and the batter who faced keeps the strike.

Runs completed, and wickets off extras

A run out doesn't always happen on a dot ball. When you choose Run Out or Obstructing, three extra questions appear:

1
Runs completed before the wicket

0 to 4. If they ran one and were run out going for the second, choose 1. The completed runs count, and go to the batter if they were off the bat.

2
The ball was

Off the bat, Bye, Leg-bye, Wide or No-ball. The runs go to the right place — a wide or no-ball also adds its penalty and doesn't count as a ball in the over.

3
Who faces the next ball?

The app suggests the usual answer, but it depends on which end the wicket was broken at, so check it and tap the other name if needed. If this was the last ball of the over, answer for the first ball of the next over — the app allows for the change of ends.

For a Stumping, you can mark the ball as a Wide — a stumping off a wide is still the bowler's wicket. Everything else defaults to 0 runs off a legal ball, so an ordinary wicket is exactly as quick as before.

The ball shows in the over strip as, for example, 1W (one run, then out), Wd W or Nb 1W.

ℹ️

Winning run first. If the batters complete the winning run and one is then run out going for another, the match was already over — so the run out doesn't count. The wicket screen tells you, and Confirm records the runs only.

The last wicket

When a wicket ends the innings — all out, or on the last ball — the New batter box disappears. Just choose how he was out and tap Confirm: the dismissal is written to the scorecard first, then the innings or match ends by itself.

Out without a ball being bowled

Tap the batter's card to open Batter off — no ball bowled. Use this when nothing happened off a delivery:

  • Retired hurt — can come back later
  • Retired out — a wicket
  • Timed out — the incoming batter wasn't ready in time; a wicket

No ball is added to the over. Wickets recorded this way still go into the fall of wickets, and if it's the last wicket, no new batter is asked for.

Retired hurt returning

At the next wicket, any retired-hurt batters are listed at the top of the new batter field. Pick one and he resumes his innings with his runs and his place in the batting order.

💡

The fall of wickets is recorded automatically. On the scorecard, batters appear in the order they came in, not the order they were out.

09

Editing Balls, Overs & Names

Undo

Tap ↩ Undo to take back the last ball. The score, both batters' runs and balls, the bowler's figures and the partnership all go back exactly. It works:

  • Mid-over — as many times as you need, one ball at a time.
  • At the end-of-over prompt — the prompt disappears and the over is open again.
  • After moving to the next over — if the new over is still empty, Undo asks to reopen the previous over; say yes, then tap Undo again to remove the ball.

Undoing a wicket puts the batter back at the crease with his exact runs and balls, removes the replacement, and clears the fall of wicket.

Editing a ball in the current over

Tap any ball in the over strip. You can change what happened on it; everything recalculates.

Editing a completed over

Tap History in the bottom bar. The over history slides up over the pad; tap an over to open it, then a ball to edit it. Tap History again to close it.

Correcting a player's name

Open the scorecard (📋 Card, or a match from Recent Results) and tap ✎ Names. Pick the player — the list is grouped by team — type the correct name and tap Save.

  • The name changes everywhere in the match: batting and bowling cards, dismissals, fall of wickets, the ball-by-ball, both innings.
  • It works on a finished match too, and the website scorecard updates straight away.
  • If both teams have a player with the same name, only the one you picked changes.
  • You can't rename someone to the name of another player in the same team — that would merge their figures.

Changing the rules mid-match

Agreed something different at the toss, or picked the wrong option at setup? Open Other · 5 → ⚙ Match settings. You can change:

  • the wide / no-ball penalty (1 or 2 runs);
  • whether wides and no-balls use up a ball;
  • free hits on or off;
  • max overs per bowler (not in a 100-ball match — the format caps it at 20 balls).
💡

A change applies from the next ball. Everything already scored keeps the rule it was scored under, so correcting a setting halfway through never rewrites the balls behind it — and the same holds if figures are rebuilt after an edit. The new rules travel with the match, so a phone taking over carries on the same way.

Reducing the overs for rain is separate, under Other · 5 → 🌧 Reduce overs.

10

Mid-Over Bowler Change

Wrong Bowler Selected

If you selected the wrong bowler at the start of an over before any balls have been bowled, tap the bowler line and select the correct bowler. All balls in the over will be attributed to the correct bowler — no split occurs.

Injury or Suspension Mid-Over

If a bowler is genuinely unable to continue mid-over, tap the bowler line and select the replacement. This creates a split over — both bowlers share the same over number, each attributed only the balls they actually bowled. For example, if Bowler A bowled 2 balls then Bowler B took over, Bowler A is credited 2 balls and their runs, Bowler B gets the remaining 4 balls and their runs. Together they complete the over.

The over summary header in the Balls tab and over history will show both names (e.g. A. Patel / R. Singh), and each ball row shows its own bowler's name.

⚠️

The replacement bowler cannot be the same bowler who bowled the previous completed over.

11

Free Hit

When Free Hits are enabled in setup, a no-ball automatically triggers a free hit for the next delivery.

Free Hit Banner
⚡ FREE HIT — batter cannot be bowled or caught

The green FREE HIT banner persists until the next legal ball is recorded. On a free hit delivery, only run outs are valid dismissals — the wicket button is still available for scoring purposes but should only be used for a run out.

To disable free hits for your league format, toggle them off on the Setup screen before starting the match.

12

Ending an Innings & Match

You rarely need to end anything yourself. An innings ends automatically when:

  • the side is all out (10 wickets, or 2 in a Super Over)
  • the overs run out (after you confirm the last over)
  • the target is reached — straight away, even on the last ball of an over

The End button

For everything else, tap End in the bottom bar:

1
In the 1st innings

End innings — start 2nd innings (a declaration, say), or end the match as No Result, Abandoned or Forfeit.

2
In the 2nd innings

Won, Lost or Draw, plus No Result, Abandoned and Forfeit. In The Hundred, Won/Lost only appear once 25 balls have been bowled to the side batting second.

Rain: reducing the overs

Open Other · 5 → 🌧 Reduce overs (rain). Enter the new overs per innings and, in a chase, the revised target your league's method gives you. The chase bar, the par score, and the bowler limits all follow the new figures.

🌧

The app's par score is a simple pro-rata guide, not DLS. Use the revised target your league's rules set — the app will score to whatever target you enter.

Catching up

If you fell behind and need to enter several overs at once, use Other · 5 → ⏱ Catch up the score.

The result

The app writes the result in the standard form, and the same words appear on the end screen, the PDF, the website and the notifications:

  • Tygerberg CC won by 6 wickets (15 balls to spare)
  • Tygerberg CC won by 1 wicket (off the last ball)
  • Greenfields CC won by 23 runs
  • … (29 balls to spare, revised target) after a rain reduction

Once a match ends it is marked completed and appears in Recent Results and on the website's fixture page.

Player of the match

When the match ends, tap 🏅 Pick player of the match on the end screen.

1
Choose the player

The top three Suggested are ranked on runs, wickets, catches, stumpings and run outs, with credit for quick scoring, tight bowling and being on the winning side. Every player from both teams is listed underneath. The suggestion is only a guide — pick whoever the captains or umpires chose.

2
Check the figures

The figures line fills itself in, e.g. 62 (41) · 2/18 · 1 ct. You can change it.

3
Save

It appears on the end screen, the app scorecard, the PDF summary page, the website scorecard and the WhatsApp text.

Later, or to change it: open the match from Recent Results and tap 🏅 POTM at the bottom of the scorecard. Clear removes it. You need signal for this, because it saves straight to the finished match. Correcting a player's name with ✎ Names updates the award too.

🏅

The button only appears once there's a result, and not for No Result or Abandoned matches.

13

Super Over

If the scores are level at the end of the 2nd innings, the app asks whether to play a Super Over.

⚡

One over a side. The side that batted second in the match bats first in the Super Over. A ⚡ SUPER OVER strip shows throughout.

Score it exactly like the match. Each side's over ends after 6 balls or 2 wickets, and the result reads, e.g., Tygerberg CC won the Super Over by 4 runs. If it ties again, another Super Over is offered.

Scorecard & PDF Export

Scorecard

Tap 📋 Card at any time. It shows both innings: batting in the order the batters came in, with proper dismissals (c Fortuin b Miller, run out (Guly)), extras, fall of wickets and bowling, plus the Manhattan and worm charts.

PDF

⬇ PDF downloads the official scorecard:

  • Page 1 — summary: teams, date and venue, the toss, the result, and a panel per innings with the total, extras, run rate, and the top three batters and bowlers.
  • Then one page per innings with the full batting, fall of wickets and bowling. A long innings carries onto another page, marked (continued).

A finished match opened from Recent Results prints its own date, toss and result — not the match you happen to be scoring.

🔒

The PDF is only available here, in the scorer app, behind the scorer PIN. The public scorecard on the website does not offer a download.

📸 Card, 📲 Text and 🔗 Share

  • 📸 Card makes a square result graphic for WhatsApp or Instagram — on a phone it opens the share sheet.
  • 📲 Text copies a WhatsApp-ready text summary.
  • 🔗 Share (during a live match) copies the link to the live score.

Offline Mode

The TCC Scorer is designed to work even without an internet connection during a match.

Connection Status Indicators
Live — saving as you score
Syncing…
Offline — updates queued
Error — check connection

Watch the status indicator in the top-right corner of the app. When offline:

  • All ball-by-ball scoring is saved locally on the device
  • Updates are queued and sent automatically when connectivity returns
  • A toast notification confirms when queued updates have been flushed
⚠️

The live display on the TCC website will not update while offline. Scoring state is preserved on the device — do not close the browser tab or clear the app cache mid-match.

Resuming After a Crash or Refresh

If the app is accidentally closed or the browser refreshes, reopen it and enter your PIN. A Resume Match? modal will appear with a summary of the last saved state. Tap Resume → to continue scoring exactly where you left off.

Handing Over to Another Scorer

If another scorer needs to take over a live match from a different device, they tap the fixture (which will show a ● Live badge) and enter the match PIN. The new phone picks up the whole match — the score, both batters, the bowler and his figures, the first innings, the toss and the bowler limits — and they carry on scoring.

From that moment the match belongs to the new device. The previous phone is locked out: it shows a full-screen Another device is scoring notice and stops sending anything. Its own copy of the scoring stays on that phone, it simply can't reach the match any more.

💡

This is why the handover is safe even if the first scorer walks off with no signal. Their phone cannot overwrite the new scorer's overs when it reconnects.

If the match changed innings just before the handover, the new device may have nobody at the crease. It will ask for the batters and bowler before you can score — that is expected, not a fault.

Leaving a Match Without Losing It

The back arrow at the top left of the scoring screen asks what you want to do once any ball has been scored:

  • Leave this screen — keep the match — goes back to the fixture list. The match stays live and you resume it from there.
  • Cancel — keep scoring — stays where you are.
  • Wrong fixture — delete this match — for when the wrong fixture was chosen. You have to type DELETE to confirm.
⚠️

Deleting removes the match for everyone, on every device, including both innings. Only use it when the wrong fixture was picked. If you simply want off the screen, use Leave this screen.

If nothing has been scored yet the match closes without asking — there is nothing to lose, and the empty entry has to clear or the fixture stays marked live.

Overs Per Side

The overs agreed at the toss are saved with the match, so a match resumed on another device still ends at the right point. If you ever resume a match and the app says the overs per side were not stored, set them under Other → Reduce overs before carrying on — otherwise the innings will not end on its own.

App Updates

The TCC Scorer updates itself automatically — no manual reinstall or cache clearing required. When a new version is available:

  • If you are not mid-match, the app reloads silently the next time you bring it to the foreground
  • If you are mid-over, a toast notification appears — tap it to apply the update at a safe moment
💡

You will always be running the latest version automatically. If you are ever unsure, close and reopen the app — the update check runs every time the app comes to the foreground.

When a Match Goes Missing

Two extra pages sit alongside the scorer for the days when something has gone wrong. Neither is part of normal scoring and neither needs a separate login beyond the scorer PIN.

Recovering scoring from a device

Open /scorer/recover.html on the device that was scoring. It reads that phone's saved match and shows you the score, the batting and bowling cards and the fall of wickets, with a button to download the whole thing as a file. It only reads — it never uploads, changes or deletes anything.

  • Use the same browser that did the scoring. A match saved in Safari is invisible to Chrome on the same phone.
  • Check every device that touched the match. If someone took over, their phone kept its own copy.
  • Do it before starting or resuming anything on that device. Starting a new match clears the saved copy.
⚠️

If a match was deleted by mistake, go to the recovery page first and before anything else. Recent deletions are kept on the device that deleted them, and they are listed at the top of that page.

Typing up a match by hand

Open /scorer/manual-scorecard.html for a match that was never scored live, or one that could not be recovered. Enter the scorer PIN, pick the fixture, then fill in each innings: the total, wickets, overs, extras, and every batter and bowler.

A running check at the bottom of the screen adds up the batters' runs plus extras and compares that to the total you entered, telling you how many runs are unaccounted for. Use it — it catches the usual slips before anything is saved.

Saving writes a completed match and sets the fixture result, so the scorecard appears on the website and the player statistics pick it up. The over-by-over view will be empty, which is expected for a match entered this way.

💡

If you are rebuilding a match from memory, the notifications sent during the play are a good source. Each wicket alert carries the score at that moment, and the innings-change alert states the target — between them you can reconstruct the fall of wickets and the first-innings total.
